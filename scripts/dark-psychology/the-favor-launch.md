# Favor: the launch (dark psychology Short, full product-launch version)

The reciprocity Short staged as a tech product launch: a keynote stage, a hero product shot, a
three-step "how it works", a benchmark chart, a Pro tier, "one more thing" and an availability line.
About 60s. The facts follow `the-favor.md` and its fact check.

**Voice:** one narrator, a calm, warm, proud launch presenter. Original voice, not an imitation.
**Locations:** @office (present day), @lab1971, @stage (black keynote stage, one spotlight, a huge
screen behind a faceless presenter in a plain dark sweater, seen from behind or in silhouette).
**Look of the product shots:** seamless black, slow turntable, a soft light sweeping across the
object, thin white sans-serif type. No real company's logo, name or product names on screen.
**Pause marks:** `[·]` 0.2–0.3s · `[beat]` 0.4–0.5s · `[hold]` 0.8–1.0s.

## What makes it read as a launch

- **The launch beats, in order:** greeting, the problem with the world, "so we built", the reveal on
  the name, how it works, a benchmark, the Pro tier, "one more thing", availability.
- **Launch vocabulary:** *introducing, three simple steps, the hardware, up to 2×, Pro, one more
  thing, available now.*
- **Product shots stay clean:** one object, black void, a light sweep, thin type, and nothing else on
  screen. That contrast with the dark message is what makes it unsettling.
- **The presenter is never shown laughing or menacing.** Pride is the tone.

## Notes

- "Favor Pro" (row 14) and "already installed in everyone" (row 16) are dramatization, not study
  results. Row 16 rests on the widely documented norm of reciprocity (Gouldner, 1960).
- The launch style is parody. Keep real company names, logos and product names off screen and out
  of the title, so the video isn't mistaken for an official ad or claimed over trademark.
