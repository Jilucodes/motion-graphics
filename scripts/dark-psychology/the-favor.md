# The Favor (dark psychology Short, keynote-reveal version)

"villain keynote": the tactic is the product, Regan's study is the lab benchmark, and the end loops
back to the opening. About 50s.

**Voice:** one narrator, calm and warm like a product presenter, a little too pleased with the product.
**New location:** @stage, a black keynote stage with a single cold spotlight and a stone plinth.

## Why this version should hold attention

- **The hook is kept** (rows 1 and 3) and **the loop is kept**: row 13 ends on "covers your shift…",
  which runs straight back into row 1.
- **The keynote frame turns a lecture into a reveal.** "Introducing… The Favor" gives the viewer a
  name to remember, and the study plays as a product benchmark instead of a citation.
- **Every key word has a picture event** (zoom, slam, cloth, bars, the unfolding contract).
- **The silent beat** (row 12) lets the viewer feel the pull of "Sure!" before the last line.
- **The register is dry, not jokey.** The narrator is pleased with a manipulation tactic, so the
  darkness comes from his calm.

## Optional defence ending

To give viewers something to act on (it helps comments and keeps the channel educational), swap
row 13 for: "Defense? [·] Say thank you. [beat] Owe nothing." Ending there breaks the loop, so the
loop version is the default.

## Source and fact check

Regan, D. T. (1971). Effects of a favor and liking on compliance. *Journal of Experimental Social
Psychology*, 7(6), 627–639.

- The participants weren't strangers on the street. They were taking part in what they were told was an
  art-rating study, and the man who gave the Coke was posing as a fellow participant (he was Regan's
  assistant). The script says "in the lab", which is accurate.
- The assistant, not Regan, asked about the raffle tickets.
- "Nearly twice as many tickets" and "even the ones who didn't like him" match the published result:
  the favor raised compliance whether or not the person liked him.
- "Ten cents" is the price given in Cialdini's account of the study in *Influence* (and tickets at
  25 cents each). Check it against the paper before publishing, or drop row 6's "Ten cents".
